## 1. Research Motivation & Problem Statement

Recent breakthroughs in generative audio—spanning latent diffusion models, autoregressive language modeling over neural audio codecs, and flow-matching architectures—have made high-fidelity synthetic music creation widely accessible. While enabling new forms of musical expression, this capability poses significant challenges for digital forensics, media provenance, copyright attribution, and synthetic content verification.

Existing academic literature and commercial detection systems frequently demonstrate high classification accuracy on closed evaluation datasets. However, these systems often fail to generalize when encountering audio generated by unseen architectures, different post-processing pipelines, or varied compression formats.

### Primary Research Question
> *"Are the patterns learned by audio classifiers genuinely intrinsic characteristics of synthetic music, or are they merely artifacts of the specific datasets and generator architectures present during training?"*

**HarmonyShield** investigates this question through rigorous Leave-One-Generator-Out (LOGO) cross-validation, YouTube ID-level leakage auditing, and a multi-method forensic ensemble that surfaces model uncertainty rather than fabricating false certainty.

---

## 2. Research Scope

- **Domain:** Binary classification between fully AI-generated music and human-created music.
- **Audio Constraints:** 10-second analysis windows, standardized at 16,000 Hz mono (with 22,050 Hz compatibility for spectral feature extraction).
- **Core Principle:** Detection models should expose disagreement and uncertainty. An `INCONCLUSIVE` verdict on out-of-distribution audio is scientifically honest and preferable to a confident misclassification.

## 4. Dataset & Leakage Prevention

### Evaluated Data Sources
1. **FakeMusicCaps (AI Class):** Synthetic music generated from Google MusicCaps captions across five contemporary architectures:
   - `audioldm2` (AudioLDM 2)
   - `stable_audio_open` (Stable Audio Open)
   - `musicldm` (MusicLDM)
   - `mustango` (Mustango)
   - `MusicGen_medium` (Audiocraft / MusicGen Medium)
2. **Google MusicCaps (Human Class):** Curated human music clips with professional descriptions, queried via YouTube IDs (`ytid`) and trimmed to strict 10.000-second windows using `ffmpeg` to eliminate keyframe duration drift.

### Group Isolation to Prevent Data Leakage
Because synthetic clips were prompted with captions originating from corresponding MusicCaps entries, splitting data purely at the file or clip level causes severe cross-set conceptual leakage (the model recognizes the musical prompt rather than acoustic generator artifacts).

To eliminate this vulnerability:
- Train and test sets are split strictly at the **YouTube ID (`ytid`) group level** using `GroupShuffleSplit` (80% train, 20% test).
- Paired human clips sharing a test `ytid` are excluded from the training split.
- A programmatic audit verified **zero intersection** between training and test `ytid` sets across all folds ($\text{Train } ytid \cap \text{Test } ytid = \emptyset$). See [`experiments/generator_generalization/LEAKAGE_AUDIT.md`](file:///D:/PROJECTS/harmonyshield/harmonyshield/experiments/generator_generalization/LEAKAGE_AUDIT.md).

## 5. Feature Engineering (Classical Pipeline)

We evaluated five feature subsets ($A$ through $E$) extracted via `librosa`:

| Set | Components | Dimension | Description |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| **A** | MFCC | 40 | 20 Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (Mean & Std) |
| **B** | Spectral | 14 | Centroid, Bandwidth, Rolloff, Flatness, Contrast across 7 octave bands |
| **C** | MFCC + Spectral | 54 | Combined timbral and spectral envelope representations |
| **D** | MFCC + Spectral + Harmonic | 80 | Adds Chroma (12 bands) and Harmonic/Percussive energy ratio |
| **E** | All Features | 85 | Adds RMS Energy, Zero-Crossing Rate, and Tempo/Onset envelope |

**Empirical Finding:** **Feature Set B (Spectral Features)** yielded the highest generalization and stability. Spectral contrast across octave bands proved to be the most influential indicator of synthetic audio generation artifacts.

## 6. Model Architectures

### Method 1 — Classical Random Forest
- **Pipeline:** `StandardScaler` followed by `RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=100, random_state=42)`.
- **Input:** 14-dimensional Feature Set B vector.
- **Artifact:** Stored in [`data/pilot_dataset/best_rf_model.joblib`](file:///D:/PROJECTS/harmonyshield/harmonyshield/data/pilot_dataset/best_rf_model.joblib) (364 KB). Fast, lightweight, and fully interpretable via feature importance inspection.

### Method 2 — ResNet18 Mel-Spectrogram Ensemble
- **Input:** 128-band Mel-spectrogram ($1024$ FFT, $512$ hop, normalized dB scale).
- **Architecture:** Torchvision ResNet18 adapted for single-channel spectrograms, replicated to 3 channels to preserve ImageNet initialization benefits, with a custom single-logit binary classification head.
- **Ensemble:** 5 frozen checkpoints trained under Leave-One-Generator-Out cross-validation. In testing, all five models evaluate the input and their sigmoid probabilities are averaged.

### Method 3 — Pretrained Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST)
- **Role:** Independent external baseline (`AI-Music-Detection/ai_music_detection_large_60s`).
- **Function:** Evaluates the complete track in 60-second windows without project-specific fine-tuning, providing an impartial reference point.

## 7. Experimental Results

### In-Domain Pilot Evaluation (AudioLDM2 vs. Human)
On an initial in-domain pilot partition (100 AudioLDM2 clips vs. 100 Human clips):
- **Feature Set B + Logistic Regression:** F1 = **0.9756**, ROC-AUC = **0.9975**
- **Feature Set B + Random Forest:** F1 = **0.9520**, ROC-AUC = **0.9850**

### Cross-Generator Generalization (Leave-One-Generator-Out)
When evaluating cross-generator robustness by holding out one complete generator from training and testing exclusively on it:

| Held-Out Generator | Classical RF F1 | Classical RF ROC-AUC | ResNet18 F1 | ResNet18 ROC-AUC |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| `audioldm2` | 0.6977 | 0.6275 | 0.6977 | 0.6275 |
| `stable_audio_open` | 0.6552 | 0.6075 | 0.6552 | 0.6075 |
| `musicldm` | 0.7843 | 0.8475 | 0.7843 | 0.8475 |
| `mustango` | 0.7018 | 0.6650 | 0.7018 | 0.6650 |
| `MusicGen_medium` | 0.7843 | 0.8250 | 0.7843 | 0.8250 |
| **Mean ± Std** | **0.7092 ± 0.0367** | **0.7152** | **0.7246 ± 0.0514** | **0.7145** |

### External Black-Box Demonstration
When evaluated on two completely external tracks (one Suno AI song and one human recording) that were never part of any training, validation, or tuning set:
- **Human Track:** Classical: Human (0.1700) | AST: Human (0.3802) | ResNet: AI (0.9389) $\rightarrow$ **INCONCLUSIVE / LOW CONFIDENCE**
- **Suno AI Track:** Classical: Human (0.0000) | ResNet: AI (0.7862) | AST: AI (0.9947) $\rightarrow$ **INCONCLUSIVE / LOW CONFIDENCE**

**Key Forensic Finding:** Classical ML overfit its training distribution and failed completely on the commercial Suno generator (classifying it as 100% human). Conversely, ResNet and AST recognized the synthetic artifacts. The ensemble engine correctly detected this severe divergence and flagged the result as **INCONCLUSIVE**, demonstrating that multi-model disagreement analysis effectively prevents false certainty.

## 11. Important Limitations & Ethical Considerations

1. **Dataset Scale:** Evaluated on a 600-clip pilot dataset ($100$ human, $500$ synthetic). While suitable for controlled cross-generator experiments, real-world deployment requires larger and more diverse training corpora.
2. **Generalization Frontier:** Generalization across commercial closed-source generators (such as Suno, Udio) remains difficult for pure handcrafted spectral models.
3. **Forensic Integrity:** Model agreement indicates consistency of observed acoustic features, not absolute ground truth. The system explicitly flags uncertain predictions as `INCONCLUSIVE`.
